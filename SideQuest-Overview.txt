# SideQuest
### An AI-powered, gamified study activation tool for neurodivergent students

**Hackathon:** Technica 2026  
**Primary target:** Best Use of Gemini API  
**Team:** 2 developers  
**Goal:** Build a polished, functional MVP within the remaining hackathon time.

---

## 1. The Problem We're Solving

Most productivity and habit-tracking apps assume that once someone knows what they need to do, they can simply start doing it.

For many students with ADHD, autism, learning disabilities, or executive-function challenges, that isn't necessarily true.

Students may struggle with:

- **Task initiation:** Knowing an assignment needs to be completed but feeling unable to begin.
- **Task paralysis:** Becoming overwhelmed by the size, complexity, or uncertainty of an assignment.
- **Transitions:** Struggling to switch from relaxing, gaming, or scrolling to studying.
- **Time blindness:** Underestimating how long tasks or breaks take.
- **Dopamine-seeking distractions:** Turning to social media or endless games for stimulation, then struggling to disengage.
- **Recovery:** Feeling discouraged after procrastinating or losing focus, making it even harder to restart.

Existing productivity apps often require users to manually create tasks, organize schedules, navigate dashboards, and maintain streaks.

**Our insight: Sometimes the last thing an overwhelmed student needs is another productivity app to manage.**

We want to reduce the effort required to start studying rather than add more steps.

## 2. Our Solution

SideQuest is a cozy, game-inspired web application that helps students transition into studying through short, intentional microgames and AI-generated next steps.

**Our concept: Play your way into focus.**

Instead of opening an app and managing a long to-do list, students can immediately start a short game designed to provide a predictable, low-pressure transition into their next activity.

Once the game ends, Google's Gemini API helps the student identify one small, achievable action to begin or resume studying.

The goal isn't to keep students playing.

**The goal is to make leaving the game and returning to real life feel achievable and rewarding.**

### Core product principles

1. **Low friction:** Minimal setup, few decisions, and no mandatory account creation.
2. **Finite gameplay:** Games have a clear endpoint rather than infinite replay loops.
3. **Adaptive support:** AI adjusts suggested actions based on the student's situation.
4. **No guilt-based motivation:** No broken-streak punishment or shame for struggling.
5. **Accessibility:** Predictable interactions, reduced-motion options, readable text, and minimal sensory overload.
6. **Real-world progress:** The ultimate reward is beginning or returning to an actual study task.

## 3. How SideQuest Works

### Step 1: Open SideQuest

The user sees a cozy, visually appealing interface with one prominent button:

**"Start a quick reset"**

There is no requirement to enter assignments, set up a schedule, or navigate a complicated dashboard.

### Step 2: Play a short microgame

The user plays a simple game lasting approximately 30–90 seconds.

Example: **The Color Garden**

- A small garden contains several flowers.
- The user finds or matches a few flowers.
- Each correct selection gives subtle visual feedback.
- After completing the objective, the game ends.
- The user receives a small reward, such as XP or a collectible star.

The game is intentionally finite, with no endless level progression.

### Step 3: Transition into studying

After the game, SideQuest asks:

**"What are you trying to work on?"**

The student can enter a short sentence:

"I have a programming assignment due tomorrow, but I don't understand pointers."

The app sends this context to Gemini.

Gemini generates an achievable first action:

"Open your assignment and identify the first question involving pointers. You don't need to solve it yet."

The student can then start that action.

### Step 4: Adapt when the student gets stuck

After the initial action, the student can choose:

- **I started!** — Continue to a slightly larger action.
- **I'm still stuck** — Generate an easier or different starting point.
- **I need a break** — Offer a short reset and an easy way to return.

Gemini adjusts its response rather than repeating the same advice.

This is the feature that makes SideQuest more than a basic game with a chatbot.

## 4. Main Features

### Feature A: Microgames

Short, self-contained activities intended to make the transition into studying feel less abrupt.

Possible games:

**The Color Garden:** Match flowers or colors in a cozy garden.

**The Bookshelf:** Sort books into matching categories or shelves.

**The Bridge Builder:** Complete a tiny pattern puzzle to build a bridge.

For the MVP, we only need **one fully functional game**.

The others can be future concepts.

### Feature B: AI Study Reentry

Gemini generates personalized next steps based on:

- What the student needs to study
- Why they're struggling
- Their current energy level, if provided
- Whether previous suggestions worked

Examples:

**Student:** "I need to write an essay, but I don't know where to begin."

**Gemini:** "Open the assignment instructions and highlight the main question you're being asked to answer."

**Student:** "I have no motivation to study calculus."

**Gemini:** "Open your notes and find one example you've already completed successfully."

The system should prioritize specific, achievable actions rather than generic motivational statements.

### Feature C: Adaptive Difficulty

If a student cannot complete a suggested action, the app offers a smaller or alternative action.

For example:

Original quest: "Solve one practice problem."

Student selects: "I'm still stuck."

New quest: "Find one practice problem and identify what information is given."

This creates an adaptive system that responds to difficulty instead of treating incomplete tasks as failure.

### Feature D: Guilt-Free Gamification

Students receive small rewards for meaningful progress.

Potential mechanics:

- XP for completing an activation game
- XP for attempting a real-world study action
- Collectible stars or decorative items
- A character or environment that gradually develops
- Positive reinforcement for restarting after interruptions

**Important:** The game should not punish missed days, demand daily engagement, or encourage endless play.

### Feature E: Recovery Mode

If the student becomes distracted or struggles to return, they can select:

**"Help me restart."**

Gemini offers a manageable reentry action without judgment.

Example:

"You don't need to make up for the last hour. Open your notes and find where you left off."

This is particularly relevant for students who experience executive-function difficulties.

We want SideQuest to feel like a cozy indie game, not a corporate productivity dashboard.

- Pixel-art-inspired illustrations

- Cozy study rooms, gardens, and magical environments

### MVP Screens

**Screen 1 — Welcome**

A cozy game environment with a prominent "Start a quick reset" button.

**Screen 2 — Microgame**

One functional matching game with simple instructions and a visible completion goal.

**Screen 3 — Study Transition**

A text input asking what the student wants to study.

**Screen 4 — AI-Generated Quest**

Displays Gemini's suggested action, with buttons to start or request an easier action.

**Screen 5 — Progress / Reward**

Shows a small XP reward and provides an option to continue studying or restart later.

We should focus on making these screens polished and functional rather than building a large dashboard.

## 6. How We're Using Gemini

Gemini is the core AI component of SideQuest.

It isn't just a chatbot. It acts as an **adaptive study transition assistant**.

### Gemini's responsibilities

**Task interpretation:** Understand what the student is trying to accomplish.

**Barrier identification:** Interpret whether the student is overwhelmed, confused, unmotivated, or struggling to begin, without making medical diagnoses.

**Quest generation:** Produce a small, concrete action the student can realistically start.

**Adaptive support:** Adjust the action when the student reports being stuck.

**Recovery assistance:** Help students restart after interruptions.

### Example API response

```json
{
  "barrier": "task_overwhelm",
  "questTitle": "Find Your Starting Point",
  "questDescription": "Open your programming assignment and identify the first question about pointers.",
  "estimatedMinutes": 2,
  "xpReward": 10,
  "encouragement": "You don't need to solve anything yet. Just find your starting point."
}
```

For the MVP, one Gemini-powered quest-generation endpoint is sufficient.

We do not need to implement a complex multi-agent architecture.

A future version could use separate planning, coaching, and scheduling agents, but that is outside tonight's scope.

## 7. Recommended Technology Stack

We want to minimize backend complexity because both developers are still building technical experience.

| Technology | Purpose |
|---|---|
| React + Vite | Frontend application |
| Tailwind CSS | Styling and responsive design |
| JavaScript | Game mechanics and UI logic |
| Google Gemini API | Personalized study quests |
| Vercel Serverless Functions | Small, secure backend endpoint |
| localStorage | Save basic progress without a database |
| Claude Code / Cursor | AI-assisted implementation |
| GitHub | Version control and collaboration |
| Vercel | Deployment |

**Why this stack?**

We can build most of the application on the frontend.

The only backend functionality we need is a small serverless endpoint that securely communicates with Gemini.

We should never expose the Gemini API key directly in frontend code.

For development, use a local serverless function or equivalent Node endpoint. Deploy the frontend and API together.

## 8. Minimum Viable Product

The complete MVP should demonstrate this user journey:

1. Student opens SideQuest.
2. Student starts and completes one short game.
3. Student enters what they're struggling to study.
4. Gemini generates a personalized action.
5. Student reports whether they could start.
6. If stuck, Gemini generates an easier action.
7. Student receives a small reward for progress.

**This is our definition of a successful build.**

Everything else is optional.

### Must-have features

- Polished landing page
- One working microgame
- Gemini API integration
- AI-generated study action
- "I'm still stuck" adaptive response
- Simple XP or reward system
- Working deployed application

### Nice-to-have features

- A second microgame
- Character customization
- Study timer
- Sound effects
- Animated progress indicators
- Persistent progress across sessions

### Do not build tonight

- Full calendar integration
- Canvas integration
- Notion integration
- Authentication
- Complex database
- Multiple autonomous AI agents
- Multiplayer features
- Advanced analytics
- Mobile-native app

These could be part of our future vision, but they would put the hackathon MVP at risk.

## 10. What Makes SideQuest Different?

Many existing productivity tools focus on planning, tracking, or rewarding completed tasks.

SideQuest focuses on **the transition between wanting to do something and actually beginning it**.

Our differentiators are:

**Low-friction entry:** Students don't need to maintain a complicated productivity system.

**Intentional gameplay:** Games are designed with clear endpoints rather than maximizing screen time.

**AI personalization:** The next step changes based on the student's actual situation.

**Adaptive recovery:** Getting stuck doesn't end the experience; the system adjusts.

**Neurodivergent-centered design:** The interface accounts for common executive-function barriers without assuming every neurodivergent student has the same needs.

We're not claiming that playing a microgame clinically improves attention or treats ADHD. Our hypothesis is that short, predictable activities combined with personalized next steps can make study transitions feel more approachable.

## 11. Hackathon Demo

Our demo should tell one simple story.

**Scenario:** A college student has a programming assignment due tomorrow. They know they need to start but feel overwhelmed.

1. They open SideQuest instead of scrolling through social media.
2. They complete a short, visually satisfying microgame.
3. They enter: "I need to work on my CS assignment, but I don't understand pointers."
4. Gemini generates a small first action.
5. They select "I'm still stuck."
6. Gemini makes the task easier and more specific.
7. The student starts their task and receives a small reward.

This demonstrates the game, the AI integration, and the adaptive functionality in one continuous flow.

### Our pitch

"Most productivity apps help students organize what they need to do. But for students with executive-function challenges, knowing what to do isn't always enough. Starting is the hard part.

SideQuest transforms the transition into studying into a short, intentional game experience. Once the game ends, Gemini generates a personalized first step and adapts if the student is still stuck.

Unlike endless games or productivity apps that demand constant management, SideQuest is designed to help students leave the screen and begin their real work."
